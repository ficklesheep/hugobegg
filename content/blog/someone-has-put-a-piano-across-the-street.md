Someone has put a piano across the street. 



I'm certainly no stranger to the sound of a piano, but I sometimes wish that I were a stranger to this one. 



The hands that play it certainly are...



Daily renditions of Für Elise have filled my room in the few months since it appeared on the side of the road, none markedly different from my own attempts, and I can barely find middle C. There are those who insist on learning a piece once they sit down rather than drag out a dusty relic of what was once hastily rehearsed...and while I'm all for basic musical education, I'm sure Elise would have been less than impressed. 



At night, it's as if the piano breathes. Drawing breath as the nondescript babble of party goers approaches, pausing, as atonal compositions fill the silence, then exhaling as they natter, giggle, and stumble away. It is not until after I've rehearsed a desperate plea of silence to stammer from my front door do I drift off between breaths. 



The mornings I don't mind as much. The piano sleeps in during the week and I've left long before it wakes up. 



On the weekends, it's up early. Excited parents hoist curious children onto the stool and revel as their polyrythmic prodigy plonks away, drawing the startled gaze of patrons in the coffee shop to its right — they can only bear it for a minute longer than I, before the child is gloriously whisked away. 



Shrill cries generally ensue. 



I often wonder when the piano might be taken away; I suspect it was meant to be temporary, but quite unlike the fascinated children, no one has come to collect it. I check it's still there everytime I leave the house, expectant and curious everytime I open the front door. 



I do hope it stays.
